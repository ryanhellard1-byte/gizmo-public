# Hellard Spectral-Dominance Law Candidate

Status: research hypothesis, not an established law of nature.

## Core statement

For a dynamical Fourier mode omega with spectral acceleration power P_omega and weighted slower-background power B_omega, define

D_omega = P_omega / (P_omega + beta B_omega).

The candidate inertial response is

mu_H = 1 - D_omega^p [1 - mu(a/a_H)],

with a_H approximately 1.1e-10 m/s^2 and mu(x)=x/sqrt(1+x^2) as the current benchmark.

The mode equation is

 a * mu_H = g_N

only as a reduced one-mode relation. In the full spectral action, reciprocal reaction terms produced by variation must also be included.

## Limits that define the candidate law

1. High acceleration: a/a_H -> infinity gives mu_H -> 1 and Newtonian/GR dynamics.
2. Isolated dominant low-acceleration mode: B_omega -> 0 gives D_omega -> 1, so a mu(a/a_H)=g_N and a^2 approximately a_H g_N in the deep regime.
3. Deep isolated circular orbit: v^4 = G M a_H.
4. Background-dominated weak subsystem: B_omega >> P_omega gives D_omega -> 0 and mu_H -> 1, suppressing the universal wide-binary enhancement of ordinary diagonal MOND.

## Empirical benchmark status

- SPARC all-points RAR fit: best a_H approximately 1.12e-10 m/s^2 in the current simplified likelihood.
- Solar-System high-acceleration limit: strongly Newtonian in current reduced tests.
- Cassini: modified-gravity MOND Q2 constraints do not map directly onto this modified-inertia construction; a full ephemeris likelihood remains required.
- Wide binaries: current two-mode action-derived benchmark predicts only a few-percent localized deviation rather than a monotonic large MOND boost.

## Fatal unresolved problem

The current p=2, beta=1, n=4 trial spectral action is not globally convex over all sampled comparable-frequency, low-amplitude configurations. The model therefore remains incomplete.

A publishable Hellard law candidate must satisfy simultaneously:

A. Variational integrability / reciprocal Hessian.
B. Positive-semidefinite kinetic-response Hessian over the declared physical domain, or a demonstrated weaker stability criterion appropriate to the nonlocal theory.
C. Correct isolated deep limit and BTFR.
D. Real SPARC likelihood without dataset-specific retuning.
E. Solar-System ephemeris constraints.
F. Individual wide-binary likelihood.
G. Universality of free fall for composite bodies.
H. Causal/consistent initial-value prescription.
I. Relativistic completion sufficient for lensing, gravitational waves, and cosmology.

Until A-I are met, use the term Hellard Spectral-Dominance Principle or Hellard-law candidate, not an established law.
