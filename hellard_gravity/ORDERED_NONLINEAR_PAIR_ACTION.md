# Ordered nonlinear pair-action completion

## Motivation

The linear spectral-mixing no-go theorem shows that a fundamental Hellard modified-inertia action cannot use linear power mixing alone while retaining both a positive low-amplitude multimode sector and an exact Newtonian high-acceleration limit.

A surviving class is a nonlinear ordered pair action. For each pair of positive frequencies, label the faster mode `f` and the slower mode `s`, so that

\[
0<y=\omega_s/\omega_f<1.
\]

Use modal powers

\[
P_f=|a_f|^2/a_H^2,\qquad P_s=|a_s|^2/a_H^2,
\]

and the isolated kinetic primitive

\[
\Phi'(P)=\mu(\sqrt P),\qquad
\mu(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}.
\]

Define the inertia-deficit primitive

\[
R(P)=P-\Phi(P),\qquad R'(P)=1-\mu(\sqrt P).
\]

A reduced two-mode action is

\[
\mathcal K_{fs}
=\alpha_f\Phi(P_f)+\alpha_s\Phi(P_s)
+\alpha_f\lambda K(y)H(P_f)G(P_s),
\]

where \(\alpha_i\propto\omega_i^{-2}\), and

\[
K(y)=(1-y^2)^2.
\]

The taper forces the environmental interaction to vanish continuously as the two frequencies become indistinguishable.

## Minimal bounded version

A parameter-light bounded choice is

\[
H(P)=1-e^{-R(P)},\qquad G(P)=1-e^{-P}.
\]

Both vanish when their mode is absent. Their derivatives vanish at high acceleration, so the interaction disappears in the Newtonian limit. Variation of the single action automatically supplies reciprocal backreaction on the slow mode, suppressed by \((\omega_s/\omega_f)^2\).

This minimal form is numerically stable over the tested two-mode amplitude/frequency grid, but its best broad wide-binary stress result is only at the several-percent level (about 7% in the current coarse scan). It is therefore retained as a useful structural proof-of-concept, not the preferred observational benchmark.

## Higher-performing bounded version

The best current reduced phenomenological completion uses

\[
H(P)=R(P)e^{-P/P_f},
\]

\[
G(P)=\left(1-e^{-P/P_0}\right)e^{-P/P_s},
\]

with a smooth ordered-frequency taper \(K(y)=(1-y^2)^2\). A coarse stress scan found representative values near

\[
P_f\sim100,\qquad P_0\sim0.2,\qquad P_s\sim300,\qquad \lambda\sim1,
\]

that reduce the worst circular wide-binary speed deviation over the tested mass/background/separation box to about 1% while preserving a positive two-mode Hessian throughout the tested domain.

These numbers are dimensionless but are **not yet theoretically derived**, so this version is not a final law. Their purpose is to prove that the no-go requirements are not mutually inconsistent.

## Important structural properties

1. **Isolated mode:** pair term vanishes and the circular MOND/RAR branch is recovered.
2. **Absent mode:** \(H(0)=G(0)=0\), so no spurious force is generated by an empty spectral channel.
3. **High acceleration:** derivatives of the pair functions vanish, restoring Newtonian response.
4. **Reciprocity:** the pair interaction comes from a single scalar action, so cross derivatives satisfy the variational symmetry automatically.
5. **Comparable frequencies:** \(K(y)\to0\) as \(y\to1\), removing the instability found in earlier comparable-frequency completions.
6. **Strong frequency hierarchy:** slow-mode reciprocal reaction carries the natural \((\omega_s/\omega_f)^2\) suppression from the kinetic weights.

## Remaining problem

The extra dimensionless shape parameters must be derived from a deeper principle or eliminated before this can reasonably be promoted as a fundamental law. The next targets are an auxiliary-memory-field derivation, a causal doubled/in-in formulation, and direct likelihood tests on individual Gaia wide binaries.
