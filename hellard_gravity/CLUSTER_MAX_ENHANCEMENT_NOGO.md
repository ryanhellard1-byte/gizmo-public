# Cluster maximum-enhancement no-go for the current Hellard branch

## Statement

The current spectral-dominance Hellard law cannot produce a larger acceleration
enhancement than its isolated low-acceleration branch.

The isolated branch is

    a_iso mu(a_iso/a_H) = g_N.

The environmental/spectral-dominance mechanism is constructed to restore Newtonian
inertia when a mode is background dominated. Therefore its effective inertia function
satisfies schematically

    mu_eff >= mu_iso

for the same trial acceleration, with equality in the isolated dominant-mode limit and
mu_eff -> 1 in the strongly background-dominated limit.

For a positive attractive force equation

    a mu_eff(a) = g_N,

increasing the inertia coefficient reduces the acceleration solution. Hence

    a_Hellard <= a_iso.

Thus the isolated MOND-like branch is the maximum acceleration enhancement available
to the current nonrelativistic Hellard mechanism.

## Observational consequence

Any system that still requires residual gravitating mass after applying an isolated
MOND-like force law cannot be solved by spectral-dominance suppression alone.

Galaxy clusters are therefore a critical obstruction. If cluster dynamics/lensing demand
more gravity than the isolated branch provides, the present Hellard v0.1 law cannot remove
that discrepancy.

## Allowed ways forward

A full theory must do one of the following:

1. accept that additional matter remains in clusters;
2. add a relativistic mediator/stress sector whose cluster contribution is not bounded by
   the nonrelativistic inertia enhancement;
3. change the isolated branch itself, which would require refitting and revalidating the
   successful galaxy relation.

Option 2 is the only route that preserves the current galaxy law while attempting a
dark-matter-free cluster completion.

## Claim restriction

Until a successful cluster mediator is constructed and tested, Hellard v0.1 is a candidate
law for low-acceleration galaxy/trajectory dynamics, not a complete replacement for all
dark matter phenomena.
