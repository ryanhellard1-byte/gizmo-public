# Galilean symmetry and test-body universality theorem

## Setup

Consider a nonrelativistic many-body action

    S = sum_a m_a [ int dt (1/2)|v_a|^2 + F[a_a] ] - int dt V({r_a-r_b}),

where:

- F is the same dimensionless/history functional for every body;
- F depends only on acceleration histories, time differences, and rotational scalar contractions;
- V depends only on relative positions;
- gravity in the test-body limit supplies a force proportional to the test mass.

## Translation invariance

Under r_a -> r_a + c, velocities and accelerations are unchanged and V is unchanged because it uses only relative positions. The action is invariant. The associated generalized total momentum is conserved for the closed conservative theory.

## Rotation invariance

If F uses only scalar contractions of acceleration-history variables and V depends only on rotational invariants of relative positions, the action is rotationally invariant. The associated generalized angular momentum is conserved.

## Galilean boosts

Under r_a -> r_a + u t,

    v_a -> v_a + u,
    a_a -> a_a.

Thus every F[a_a] is exactly boost invariant. The Newtonian kinetic term changes only by the standard total-derivative/boundary contribution. Hence the complete action is Galilei invariant in the usual quasi-invariant sense and possesses the corresponding boost/center-of-mass Noether charge.

## Universality of free fall in the test-body limit

Because the kinetic functional appears with an overall factor m, its Euler-Lagrange inertial term is proportional to m. A gravitational test force is also proportional to m. Therefore m cancels from the test-body equation. The trajectory is independent of test mass and composition provided the same universal F applies.

This establishes weak-equivalence-principle behavior at the level of the effective point-particle theory. It does not prove strong-equivalence-principle behavior for self-gravitating bodies.
