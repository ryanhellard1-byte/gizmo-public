# Causality and initial-value status

## Current conclusion

The present Hellard modified-inertia constructions are stationary, time-nonlocal effective actions written most naturally in the frequency domain. A real single-history action of this type is time-symmetric. By itself, it does **not** provide a retarded causal initial-value law.

This is a genuine unresolved requirement, not a numerical detail.

## Why the issue appears

A time-translation-invariant quadratic/nonlinear spectral functional depends on the complete trajectory through Fourier amplitudes or equivalent two-time kernels. Ordinary Hamilton's principle is a boundary-value variational principle. The resulting nonlocal Euler-Lagrange equation generally depends on both past and future portions of a trajectory unless a separate causal prescription is supplied.

## Known route that remains available

Causal effective equations can be obtained with doubled variables / in-in (Schwinger-Keldysh-type) variational formalisms or related initial-value stationary-action constructions. In classical mechanics, Galley-type doubled-history actions provide a variational formulation compatible with initial data. In quantum/effective field theory, Schwinger-Keldysh prescriptions generate retarded equations for expectation values.

That means time nonlocality is not automatically inconsistent with causality, but the causal prescription must be part of the theory. It cannot be silently inferred from a time-symmetric single-history action.

## Hellard research requirement

A publishable fundamental completion must therefore do one of the following:

1. derive the spectral modified-inertia action as an effective closed-system reduction with a well-defined causal in-in prescription; or
2. introduce explicit auxiliary/memory degrees of freedom whose local initial-value evolution reproduces the required spectral response after they are integrated out.

The auxiliary-field route is currently preferred because it offers a direct stability, degree-of-freedom, and Cauchy-evolution test.

## Kill criterion

If no causal initial-value completion can reproduce the required isolated-galaxy limit and environmental spectral suppression without introducing unstable modes, acausal poles, or unacceptable dissipation, the Hellard modified-inertia branch must be rejected as a fundamental law.
